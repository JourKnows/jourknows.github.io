Super El Niño

By: Rjay Khian V. Cuizon

0

Scientists at the University of Chicago, USA, report an estimate of 451,000 people will result in fatalities in the first nine months due to the emerging “Super El Niño” that started in June.

A “Super El Niño”, according to Columbia University, is an informal label for an exceptionally strong phase of the El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO), occurring when sea surface temperatures in the central and equatorial Pacific Ocean rise 2°C or more above normal.

According to Climate Impact Lab’s forecast of heat-related deaths spanning from September 2026 to February 2027, the southern regions of the globe are the most affected.

Nearly half of those lives are in Nigeria, with an estimated 31,400; 17,500 in Sudan, 10,000 in Niger, and 8,000 in Chad.

In South East Asia, the Super El Niño is projected to cause an additional 19,400 deaths in the Philippines, Cambodia, Vietnam, and Thailand, and 19,300 casualties in Indonesia. Other regions include India, with 15,800, and Brazil, with 13,300.

The Climate Impact Lab temperature anomaly report highlights regions where extreme heat is projected to cause the most deaths and identifies areas where immediate action could save the most lives.

Targeted action could reduce the number of deaths by tens of thousands, according to the Climate Impact Lab report.
