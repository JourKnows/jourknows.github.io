Paris is
on its feet.

Students, workers, and drivers are filling the streets of France again. The anger is about school budgets, fuel, and the cost of staying afloat. If it sounds familiar, it should.

01 · Right now

A movement that moves in waves.

Since late September, strikes and marches have rolled through France. High school blockades spread across regions, universities joined, and the Yellow Vests were called back for October 17.

03 · It has been a long time

237 years of a nation that refuses to stay quiet.

France did not just protest. It made protest part of its identity.

04 · The long view

Years since 1789

Each wave answered the same question: who pays when the state and the market squeeze ordinary people?

05 · Why Filipinos must care

This is not someone else’s fight.

We share the same squeeze

Fuel hikes, frozen wages, and rising prices drive French anger. Filipino households know the pump price and the grocery bill just as well.

Youth and schools

French students are marching for classroom resources. Our campuses face crowded rooms and tight budgets, and campus press is often the first to say so.

Accountability is universal

Filipinos filled the streets against flood control corruption. The question is the same: where did the public money go?

Our people are there

Filipinos live, study, and work across France and Europe. Strikes ground flights, halt trains, and shape OFW livelihoods.

Our history rhymes

Rizal and the Propagandists absorbed Europe’s revolutionary ideas. Liberté, égalité, fraternité echoes in our own struggle for dignity.

06 · The question
When a people stops asking permission to be heard, the whole world learns what is possible.

France has asked its question for 237 years. The Philippines has asked its own, from 1896 to EDSA to today. Watching Paris is a way of learning how to ask ours louder.
