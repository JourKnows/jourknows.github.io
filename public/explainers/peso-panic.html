WORLD 63-1
PESO PANIC

The peso just broke ₱63 to the dollar for the first time ever. Walk with Juan to see what that does to prices.

STAGE 1: THE CLIMB TO ₱63

  • Jul 24: record intraday low of ₱61.845
  • Aug 28: first dip below ₱62
  • Sep 1: closes at ₱62.40
  • Oct 8: record close, about ₱62.90
  • Oct 15: closes at ₱63.06 (high of ₱63.15). It is the peso’s 27th record low this year.
SRC: GMA News, Inquirer, BAP data

STAGE 2: WHY IT KEEPS FALLING

  • The peso has lost about ₱5.21 since the US-Israeli war on Iran began Feb 28.
  • Oil climbed back above $100 a barrel in July.
  • Reserves (GIR) fell to $99.996B in September, a three-year low.
  • Odds of a US rate hike rose after a hot inflation print.
SRC: GMA News, Khaleej Times, Reuters via GMA

STAGE 3: IMPORTED INFLATION

The PSA says petroleum and electronics are top imports. A weaker peso makes every dollar-priced import cost more pesos.
  • A $100 import cost ₱5,728 at ₱57.28 (Nov 2022).
  • At ₱63.06 it costs ₱6,306, about 10.1% more.
Think tank Ibon calls a record-low peso plus high oil prices a “double whammy.” SRC: Khaleej Times, Tribune; ratio computed from rates

STAGE 4: PRICES CLIMB AGAIN

Inflation hit 7.2% in September, up from 6.1% in August. That is the fastest since March 2023 and well above the 2–4% target. Food inflation jumped to 6.7% from 4.6%.

STAGE 5: WHO FEELS IT

  • Vegetables and root crops rose 10.7% yearly in September, after falling 3.4% in August.
  • Oil firms announced a gasoline hike of up to ₱4.30 per litre.
  • Transport inflation peaked at 21.4% in April.
  • Bright spot: families receiving dollar remittances get more pesos per dollar.
SRC: GMA News, Philstar, Khaleej Times, Gulf News

STAGE 6: WHAT TO WATCH

  • The US Fed’s policy meeting next week.
  • Any BSP move to slow the slide. It has so far intervened only when moves turn sharp and disorderly.
  • Whether the peso holds above ₱63 or recovers.
