The Supreme Court (SC) affirmed Friday, Oct. 30, that men can also be victims of sexual violence, stressing that gender should not determine the protection and recognition afforded to survivors.

In a 17-page decision penned by Senior Associate Justice Marvic Leonen, the SC upheld the conviction of a woman for committing acts of lasciviousness against her then 11-year-old nephew in 2018.

The Court also rejected the woman’s argument that the child had consented to the sexual act, noting that he was below the legal age of consent at the time.

“The age of sexual consent when the incident happened in 2018 was still 12 years old before it was raised to 16 years old with the passage of Republic Act No. 11648 in 2022. Thus, AAA280727, an 11-year-old child at the time of the incident, is deemed unable to give consent,” the SC said.

The victim was later diagnosed with post-traumatic stress disorder resulting from the sexual abuse, according to the Court.

The woman was sentenced to imprisonment ranging from nine years, four months and one day to 16 years, five months and nine days and ordered to pay the victim ₱250,000 in damages.

The SC also clarified that the offense was statutory acts of lasciviousness under Article 336 of the Revised Penal Code, as the definition of rape under Article 266-A at the time did not cover the circumstances of the case.

The Court noted that Republic Act No. 11648, enacted in 2022, expanded the definition of rape through gender-neutral language, including the term “any person”, instead of limiting the provision to male perpetrators.

“When laws are not gender-neutral, male victims suffer twice: once at the hands of their abuser, and second through the judicial system,” the SC said.

The SC emphasized that sexual violence inflicts harm regardless of gender, calling for equal protection for all survivors.
