The Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) has adopted a Philippine-led declaration to improve reading and math skills among young people in the region, the Department of Education (DepEd) said Monday.

Southeast Asian leaders approved the ASEAN Declaration on Foundational Learning on Nov. 15 during the 49th ASEAN Summit and Related Summits.

In a statement, Education Secretary Juan Edgardo “Sonny” Angara said the declaration makes early education a “shared priority” across the region.

“Building a strong, competitive region starts with the basics: giving every child a solid grip on reading and math from the very beginning,” Angara said.

“This declaration goes beyond high-level policy; it is our shared commitment across Southeast Asia to make sure every young learner gets the early support they need to succeed,” he added.

The Philippines will now draft a roadmap for programs under the ASEAN Work Plan on Education 2026-2030, including the ASEAN Academic Recovery and Catch-Up Program, to address learning gaps among students.

Angara said the declaration supports existing efforts, including the revised K-10 and Senior High School curricula and the Academic Recovery and Accessible Learning Program.

The declaration began as a proposal to ASEAN leaders in February 2026 and went through regional consultations before its adoption.
