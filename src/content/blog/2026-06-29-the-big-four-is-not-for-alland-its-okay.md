For many Filipino students, entering college is less than choosing where to study and more of where does my life begin. Many of them have their eyes fixed on four big names: University of the Philippines, Ateneo de Manila University, De La Salle University, and the University of Santo Tomas.

In one click or one email, months of review classes and sleepless nights for these four distill into a single statement on a gadget screen: "Congratulations!" or "Thank you for your interest."

Some think that acceptance to the Big Four guarantees success, and when rejection greets them instead, they feel that opportunities have already closed—but does it really?

There is no denying the prestige that surrounds the Big Four. Their excellence has been recognized in and out of the Philippines, and it is usually just the four of them switching places at the top of the rankings.

Perhaps that is why many senior high school graduates also put these four at the top of their rankings when choosing where to study in college.

Though a couple thousand do successfully begin their journey in these institutions, there are hundreds of thousands more who are left to settle elsewhere in private or state universities.

According to the Commission on Higher Education (CHED), more than 3.8 million students are enrolled in higher education institutions nationwide, with only a fraction coming from the Big Four.

If success only belonged to these campuses, then the country would only have a few professionals. Instead, our classrooms, hospitals, courtrooms, newsrooms, and businesses are filled with graduates from colleges that don't always make the loudest buzz.

It may be in Diliman, Katipunan, Taft, España, or a uni kilometers away, students will inevitably face the same impossible deadlines, demanding professors, failed scores, and complete uncertainty.

Seeing these similar challenges, students may have been answering the wrong question all along. It is not "Which school guarantees my future?" but, "Who will I become wherever I study?"

A university can provide the opportunities, yet it is up to students how to seize them for personal growth. May it be leadership in orgs, confidence in competitions, curiosity in research, or character in work, these aspects are not reserved for four universities alone.

Research from the Philippine Institute for Development Studies found that positive college experiences, which includes engagement inside and outside the classroom, are associated with better employability and life satisfaction.

Eventually, every graduate arrives at the same job market wherein resumes can change the entire conversation. In this game, the school name is only a line of text in the wider picture of shifting perspectives.

While a university's name can open talks, it is rarely enough to sustain one. Employers look for those who can solve problems, communicate effectively, adapt to change, and continue to learn after formal education.

What students build during college may matter far more than the names on the gates of the school where they are from. It's the intangibles of life that makes a student effective in college and in the outside world.

The Big Four deserve every accolade they have earned. Their wide alumni network came from opening doors for generations of students and they will continue to do so.

However, they are not the only places where life begins. Success is not limited to four gates—it simply starts with walking into whatever school opened for you.
