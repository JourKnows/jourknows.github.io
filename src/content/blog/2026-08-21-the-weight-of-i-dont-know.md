In every choice made, there is always a lingering feeling of uncertainty, wrapped and disguised as relief of finally making a decision. While many students confidently declare their future with a dream profession, not everyone has an answer ready. Yet, behind every smile and certainty of others, there are still students who freeze and tremble when asked with a simple question, "*What do you want to be after high school?”* For them, the future is not a clear destination, but an unanswered question that adds weight with each passing day. 

Most students struggle when choosing a career. For many, making the right decision is a thin line between practicality and passion. Whether they choose to do what they love or question where they belong.

Furthermore, their career mainly changes based on who asks the question. When asked by their parents, they would choose a career they believe would make them proud; when asked by their friends, they might mention something interesting; and when asked by teachers, they may simply give an answer that sounds practical. In the end, they are not necessarily lying–they are still trying to figure out which answer truly belongs to them.

Like an adventurer stuck at the crossroads, they see countless signs pointing in different directions. They get confused on which path they should choose, not because they lack ambition–but because they are still waiting for the calling that truly feels theirs.

However, the hardest part is not the absence of an answer, but the feeling that they are supposed to have one already. As graduation comes closer, the question becomes frequent from parents, teachers, and friends. With every question the pressure grows even bigger. While others move with certainty, they continue to stay at the crossroads, afraid they would choose a path they didn’t want to go in the first place.

Sometimes, they envy those who seem to have their future sorted out. They watch classmates talk about their career or profession with excitement. Meanwhile, they are confused about which voice to listen to, whether they should choose something they love, what they are good at, or what others think is best for them.

But not having an immediate answer does not mean being unprepared. Sometimes, It just means that students are still learning about themselves–their interests, strengths, and ambitions they truly want to pursue. While others are already holding their maps to get out of the crossroads, some need more time to understand where they belong.

Perhaps, ‘I don’t know’ doesn’t mean an empty answer. It means that there is still something left to discover. 

Because in the end, whether they choose something practical or not, what truly matters is that decision belongs to them and not borne out of pressure and influence. Maybe ‘I don’t know’ is not the end of the journey, but the beginning of it.
