House prosecutor Rep. Terry Ridon on Monday said recent cyber libel complaints against media personalities and journalist Philip Lustre Jr. pose a threat to press freedom.

Ridon said the House prosecution panel would support the media in light of the complaints.

“The House Prosecution Panel is expressing its concern over the series of cases that were filed against the media, including broadcast networks like GMA 7 and journalist Philip Lustre. This is an attack on press freedom,” said Ridon.

On Oct. 23, Davao City Rep. Paolo “Pulong” Duterte filed cyber libel complaints against GMA Network executives and journalists.

The following day, Quezon City Police District (QCPD)  officers arrested Lustre during a news forum over an alleged cyber libel case filed by Atty. Manases “Mans” Carpio.

Ridon said there should be limits on filing cases against journalists.

“It speaks volumes about how [the Dutertes] actually look at the media, and there is historical precedent on it,” he said, citing cases against media networks such as ABS-CBN and Rappler during the term of former President Rodrigo Duterte.

Ridon also called out the Philippine National Police (PNP) for a “double standard” in serving warrants, noting that police have made no attempt to arrest Vice President Sara Duterte despite her pending grave threats case at the Quezon City court.

Ridon said he was open to co-filing a House resolution for a legislative inquiry into the incidents, echoing similar calls from ACT Teachers Party-list Rep. Antonio Tinio.
