In one tragic event, the hopes and dreams of two young men and their families were crushed. That was the unfortunate circumstance of promising basketball Blue Eagle standouts Rene Baterbonia and Divine Adili, who regrettably drowned in what was called a team-building activity in Dipaculao, Aurora.

The incident has sparked investigations by the DOJ, NBI, and CIDG. However, what is troubling is not only the loss of two students, but also the possibility that the people responsible for their safety have let them down.

Safety is always spoken of as a priority. It sounds very promising. It is included in all the manuals by educational institutions. It is discussed at all the orientations by coaches. Safety is mentioned by administrators every time after a crisis. But priorities are changeable; responsibilities are not. When students are under the care of the school and the coach, the responsibility for protecting their lives comes first.

The proof that this tragedy needs accountability is the following: why did the children get involved in something they could foresee as dangerous? As reported by local authorities in the media, the children participated in the activity too far from shore, despite resort employees warning them about the sea's instability. If it really was like that, it would not be just bad luck anymore. It would be about the judgment, and the leadership is all about the judgment.

Proponents of silence in institutions frequently point out that accidents can occur. Sure, they can. Storms emerge. Currents change. Humans err. However, modern concepts of safety are based on the understanding that most disasters occur not because danger goes unnoticed but because warnings are ignored. In aviation, medicine, engineering, and even sports management, the first question after a tragedy is not "What is wrong?" but "Which warning signs have been disregarded?"

If two young people drown during an organized event, then society has the right to find out whether there were any measures taken before the first participant jumped into the water.

These repercussions go beyond this particular institution or basketball program. Across the nation, colleges tout their camps, retreats, leadership training seminars, athletic training sessions, and other team-building activities as places where growth is supposed to take place. In most cases, they do so responsibly. However, this case sheds light on a problematic culture that has at times misunderstood challenge as character building. Our society tends to glorify toughness and endurance, and rewards individuals who stretch themselves beyond what they are capable of.

At some point, some individuals begin confusing the two concepts and end up believing that the harder an experience is, the more beneficial it is bound to be. History has proven otherwise time and again. Growth involves challenge, but does not necessitate recklessness.

But the most troubling aspect of this tragedy lies in the imbalanced nature of power in this story. Athlete students are raised to be obedient to authorities. The players trust their coaches since they hold the keys to success. It is the duty of each adult leader to ensure that he does what is right and what is expected of him. The more power he has, the bigger his responsibilities are. A student should never have doubts about their safety when following instructions. 

But there is an even quieter tragedy underneath all the media noise. Of course, public attention was mostly drawn to the need to investigate this case legally. But the main characters in this story are two families whose futures have been irreversibly changed. One family lost a son who was on the brink of his promising university career. The other lost a young man who had crossed entire continents in search of an opportunity in sports. It is not the lack of talent and willpower that made their dreams sink.

The latest events serve to underscore the gravity of the situation. The Department of Justice ordered the formation of an NBI task force to investigate. According to the CIDG, the agency has been looking into possible violations that would include murder and anti-hazing laws, depending on the evidence collected. The local government has also examined the circumstances of the event. This does not mean anything has gone wrong, but it shows that the issues raised are serious enough to warrant the highest-level investigations.

It cannot be overlooked that this is ironic. The team-building activity was intended to foster trust and bonding, and it culminated in an incident that highlighted the consequences of negligence. When safety is not the priority, institutions risk becoming no more than structures that collapse under their own weight.

Team-building activities are supposed to bring people together and foster trust and cohesion. But in cases where safety is not the priority, it could end up breaking those people. It is true that institutions measure success by trophies, championship cups, and prestige. However, the true test of any school, coach, or organization is not how many victories it produces. It is how faithfully it protects the people entrusted to its care.

Rene Baterbonia and Divine Adili deserved futures, not investigations. They deserved mentorship, not uncertainty. They deserved protection, not preventable danger. If their deaths leave the nation with any obligation, it is this: never allow the language of excellence to excuse the abandonment of responsibility. Achievement built on neglected safety is not excellence at all. It is a failure wearing the uniform of success.
