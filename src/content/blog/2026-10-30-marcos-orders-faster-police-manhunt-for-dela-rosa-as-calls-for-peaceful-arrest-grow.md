President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. ordered police Saturday to intensify efforts to arrest Sen. Ronald “Bato” dela Rosa, according to Malacañang.

Presidential Communications Office Undersecretary Claire Castro said Marcos told police to act quickly and that the government would continue efforts to enforce the law.

“Mas mabilis at agresibong pagkilos ng mga pulis, ’yan ang utos ng Pangulo. Ipatupad ang batas at hulihin at dapat na hulihin,” Castro said.

The Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) announced a ₱50 million reward on Oct. 23 for information leading to Dela Rosa’s capture.

Philippine National Police (PNP) chief Gen. Jose Melencio Nartatez Jr. also gave Dela Rosa one week to surrender and warned that those hiding the senator could face criminal charges for providing him shelter.

Meanwhile, Senate Minority Leader Alan Peter Cayetano appealed to Marcos and concerned government agencies to work with Dela Rosa’s legal counsel toward a peaceful resolution.

“The Minority stands with our colleague and is ready to help facilitate the discussion, confident that our institutions can uphold the law, protect his rights, and pursue justice without needless loss of life,” Cayetano said.

However, DILG Secretary Jonvic Remulla maintained that Dela Rosa should surrender, saying Interpol had issued a red notice for the senator.

“He has a valid red notice from Interpol. We will arrest him and accord him all that is due under the law,” Remulla said. “It is best he surrender,” he added.

Dela Rosa has not appeared in public since May 14, when he left the Senate with Sen. Robinhood Padilla as authorities moved to enforce the International Criminal Court arrest warrant.

The warrant is tied to allegations of crimes against humanity, including murder, linked to former President Rodrigo Duterte’s administration and its anti-drug campaign.

Officials said the ICC warrant is enforceable, citing the court’s finding of reasonable grounds to believe Dela Rosa was allegedly responsible as an indirect co-perpetrator in at least 32 murders in the Philippines between July 2016 and April 2018.
