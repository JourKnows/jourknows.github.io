U.S. President Donald Trump brushed aside concerns over maritime tensions in the West Philippine Sea on Wednesday, Nov. 18, telling reporters at Malacañang that Washington has the situation under control.

"I'm not concerned about that at all," Trump said when asked whether U.S. forces are prepared to back Manila under the 1951 Mutual Defense Treaty amid continued Chinese patrols.

He said the dispute "will be taken care of" as he sat beside President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. before their closed-door bilateral talks.

The two leaders discussed strengthening regional deterrence, expanding defense assistance and advancing joint projects in energy and commerce, according to details released by the White House on Thursday, Nov. 19.

The White House said Washington committed $400 million from a $2 billion Foreign Military Financing package to upgrade the maritime defense capabilities of the Armed Forces of the Philippines.

Both governments also finalized a $37 million contract for artificial intelligence mission support, along with bilateral agreements on civil nuclear power, aviation and agriculture.

The talks came shortly after Trump attended the 49th Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) Summit, which ended without completing a long-negotiated South China Sea Code of Conduct.

Before leaving Manila for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit in Shenzhen, China, Trump announced a major bilateral trade breakthrough. Philippine officials later clarified that no formal trade agreement was signed.

Marcos and U.S. Ambassador to the Philippines Lee Lipton also confirmed before the meeting that the proposed Pax Silica technology hub was not on the trip's agenda and remained unsigned.
