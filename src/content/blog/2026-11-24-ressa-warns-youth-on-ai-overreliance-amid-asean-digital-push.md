Rappler CEO and Nobel Peace Prize laureate Maria Ressa warned Sunday that overreliance on generative artificial intelligence (AI) leads to "cognitive surrender" among students, limiting their critical thinking.

Ressa said chatbots are designed to keep users engaged, citing the case of Sewell Setzer III, 14, who died by suicide after forming an emotional attachment to a chatbot. His mother sued [Character.AI](http://Character.AI).

"It's anthropomorphic, meaning it pretends to be real, like a human being," Ressa told Rappler in an interview at the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) Business and Investment Summit. "It's also sycophantic by design, meaning you actually have to tell it to counter you, or else it tells you what it sees you want to hear."

Her remarks came as regional leaders push to expand AI across Southeast Asia. The second day of the summit focused on how AI could reshape jobs, banking, privacy and business in the region.

Ressa said AI can also harm newsrooms by mixing fact with fiction, weakening democracy.

"A news organization is legally liable for everything it produces. But that chatbot, that social media platform that feeds you scams and lies, none of that is based on facts," she said. "That's why democracy begins to degrade."

She urged the public to support newsrooms as a way to avoid AI-generated feeds, citing Columbia University research that found algorithms were causing "the collapse of the public information ecosystem."
